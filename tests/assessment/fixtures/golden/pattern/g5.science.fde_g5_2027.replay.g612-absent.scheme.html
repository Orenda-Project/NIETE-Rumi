Section C (58)
Q3 (a) Answer any eight of the following nine questions briefly.
1.It gives the body its shape and holds it up, it protects soft organs such as the heart and lungs (inside the ribs), and it helps us move together with the muscles.
Name the three main jobs of the skeletal system.
K2 gives the body shape and supportK2 protects soft organs, with one example (ribs: heart and lungs; skull: brain)K1 helps the body move, working with the muscles
2.Running makes our muscles work harder, so they need more oxygen. We breathe faster to take in more oxygen and to get rid of the extra carbon dioxide.
Why do we breathe faster after running?
K1 the muscles work harderK2 they need more oxygenK1 faster breathing takes in more oxygenK1 and removes more carbon dioxide
3.A contagious disease is caused by germs and spreads from one person to another, e.g. flu or measles. A non-contagious disease does not spread from person to person, e.g. diabetes.
Write two differences between contagious and non-contagious diseases. Give one example of each.
K1 contagious: spreads from person to personK1 non-contagious: does not spreadK1 contagious diseases are caused by germsK1 a correct contagious example (flu, measles, chickenpox, TB)K1 a correct non-contagious example (diabetes, heart disease)
4.Microorganisms are tiny living things that can be seen only with a microscope. Yeast makes bread dough rise, and bacteria turn milk into yoghurt.
What are microorganisms? Write two ways in which they are useful to us.
K2 tiny living things seen only with a microscopeK1.5 one useful job: yeast makes dough rise, bacteria make yoghurt, decomposers, medicinesK1.5 a second, different useful job
5.A food chain shows one path of who eats whom, e.g. grass rabbit fox. A food web is many food chains joined together, so one animal can eat, or be eaten by, more than one living thing.
How is a food web different from a food chain?
K2 food chain: one path of feedingK2 food web: many food chains joined togetherK1 in a web an animal has more than one food or more than one enemy
6.Physical: melting butter, freezing water, cutting paper. Chemical: burning wood, baking a cake.
Sort these into physical and chemical changes: melting butter, burning wood, freezing water, baking a cake, cutting paper.
K1 melting butter: physicalK1 burning wood: chemicalK1 freezing water: physicalK1 baking a cake: chemicalK1 cutting paper: physical
7.A mirror has a very smooth, shiny surface that sends light back evenly, so it forms an image of our face. Wood is rough and dull; it scatters and absorbs light, so no image forms.
Why can we see our face in a mirror but not in a wooden board?
K1 a mirror is smooth and shinyK2 it reflects light back evenly and forms an imageK1 wood is rough and dullK1 it scatters or absorbs light, so there is no image
8.When the bell is struck it vibrates. The vibrations pass to the air around it and travel through the air as sound waves until they reach our ears, where the eardrum vibrates and we hear the sound.
How does the sound of the school bell reach our ears?
K1 the bell vibratesK2 the vibrations travel through the air (sound waves)K1 they reach the earK1 the eardrum vibrates and we hear
9.Water with dirt and salts in it conducts electricity. With wet hands, current can pass through the water into our body and give us an electric shock.
Why is it dangerous to touch an electric switch with wet hands?
K2 water (tap water, sweat) conducts electricityK2 current can pass into the bodyK1 an electric shock or serious injury
Q3 (b) Answer any two of the following questions comprehensively.
1.In the mouth the teeth chew the food and saliva softens it. The food pipe carries it to the stomach, which churns it and mixes it with digestive juices. In the small intestine digestion is finished and the useful food passes into the blood. The large intestine takes water from what is left, and the waste leaves the body.
Describe the journey of food through the digestive system, from the mouth to the large intestine. Say what happens in each part.
K2 mouth: teeth chew, saliva softens the foodK1 food pipe carries food to the stomachK2 stomach churns the food and mixes it with digestive juicesK2 small intestine: digestion finished, food absorbed into the bloodK2 large intestine: water taken back, waste leaves the body
2.Smoke from vehicles, factories and burning rubbish pollutes the air. It causes coughing, asthma and other breathing problems, and smog that harms plants and animals. We can plant trees, walk or use buses instead of cars, and stop burning rubbish.
What causes air pollution? Explain how it affects our health and our surroundings, and suggest three ways to reduce it.
K2 two causes: smoke from vehicles, factories, burning rubbish or cropsK2 health: coughing, asthma, breathing problems, sore eyesK2 surroundings: smog, harm to plants and animals, dirty buildingsK3 three ways to reduce it, 1 each (plant trees, walk or share transport, do not burn rubbish)
3.Light travels in straight lines. When an opaque object blocks it, a dark area forms behind the object: its shadow. In the morning the Sun is low in the east, so the flagpole's shadow is long and points west. At noon the Sun is high and the shadow is shortest. In the evening the Sun is low in the west and the shadow is long again, pointing east.
Explain how a shadow is formed. How does the shadow of a flagpole change from morning to noon to evening?
K2 light travels in straight linesK2 an opaque object blocks the light, leaving a dark area behind itK2 morning: Sun low in the east, shadow long and pointing westK1 noon: Sun high, shadow shortestK2 evening: Sun low in the west, shadow long and pointing east
4.A magnet attracts things made of iron, steel or nickel and has a north and a south pole. Stroke the nail many times with one pole of a magnet, always in the same direction, lifting the magnet away at the end of each stroke; the nail then picks up pins. Magnets are used in compasses, in fridge doors and in cranes that lift scrap iron.
What is a magnet? Explain how you can turn an iron nail into a magnet, and write three uses of magnets in daily life.
K1 a magnet attracts iron, steel or nickelK1 it has a north and a south poleK1 stroke the nail with one pole of a magnetK1 many times, in the same direction onlyK1 lift the magnet away at the end of each strokeK1 test: the nail now picks up pins or clipsK3 three uses, 1 each (compass, fridge door, cranes, toys, speakers)
5.Current flows from the cell, through the wires and the bulb, and back to the cell; the path must be complete, and the bulb lights. Opening the switch makes a gap, so the circuit is broken, the current stops and the bulb goes off. Plastic is an insulator: it does not let current pass, so it protects us from electric shocks.
Explain how electricity flows in a simple circuit made of a cell, a switch, wires and a bulb. What happens when the switch is opened? Why are electric wires covered with plastic?
K2 current flows from the cell through the wires and bulb and back to the cellK1 the path must be complete (closed)K1 the bulb lights when current flows through itK2 switch opened: a gap breaks the circuit, current stops, the bulb goes offK2 plastic is an insulator; it does not let current passK1 it protects us from electric shocks
