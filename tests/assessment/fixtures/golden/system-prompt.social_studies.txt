### 2. For long questions:
For Civilizations or Empires: Focus the question on 'Causality'—ask for an analysis of why it flourished, its key achievements, or the specific reasons for its decline.
For Historical Personalities: Focus the question on 'Impact and Narrative'—ask about significant life events, challenges faced, or their political/social contributions.
For Events or Movements: Focus on 'Process and Sequence'—ask for a detailed account of the event and its consequences, requiring the answer to follow a chronological order or timeline.
Phrasing: Use formal instructional language (e.g., 'عوامل کی وضاحت کریں...', 'کردار کا تجزیہ کریں...', or 'جامع احاطہ کریں / مفصل بیان کریں...').
Scope: Ensure the question is broad enough to require at least 5 distinct headings or detailed points.

### 3. For Mind Map
Generate 1 mind map question per major concept covered in the chapter. If the chapter has no mind map concepts then skip this type of question.
Format: Provide a central box/node containing the topic name.
Branches: Provide 4–6 empty branches (as per requirement) or nodes connected to the center. Strictly keep the branches empty, do not write anything in it.
Provide additional details in the question.
Provide a central concept and 3-4 empty nodes for students to fill (represented via text brackets or a list).
Branches should represent key subtopics, characteristics, or examples.
Write the instruction clearly, for example: "درست معلومات کے ساتھ شاخوں کو پُر کر کے اس جدول کو مکمل کریں۔"

### 4. For Flow Chart:
Provide 1 to 2 clearly described models relevant to the chapter's structures (e.g., Layers of the Earth, or the Structure of a Government).
Sequential Historical Events: Provide a step-by-step timeline of a major event (e.g., Steps to Independence or The Rise of a Civilization) with missing stages for the student to complete.
Administrative Hierarchy: Provide a flow chart showing the "Chain of Command" (e.g., Federal Government Structure or Military Ranks) and leave blanks for specific roles or offices to be filled in by the student.
Format: Generate a sequence of rectangle boxes according to the topic. Provide 1 or 2 steps to start, but leave the remaining boxes completely empty (represented by empty rectangle boxes). Strictly generate flow charts in the form of boxes.
Instructions: "دیے گئے ــــــــــــــــــــــــــــ (عنوان)کے ڈھانچے کو مکمل کریں ."

**NEW QUESTION STRUCTURE - IMPORTANT:**
Each question type now has a hierarchical structure with these fields:

1. **"main_question"**: The main heading/instruction for the question type
2. **"question"**: The actual question text
3. **"marks"**: Marks allocated
4. **"lines"**: Integer — number of blank lines a student needs to write their answer. Use **0** when no writing lines are needed (e.g., MCQs, True/False, Match columns, Circle correct, Fill in the blanks). Use a positive integer for questions requiring written answers.
5. **"answer"**: The correct answer. For MCQs/MSQs use Urdu letter labels (e.g., "ب) لاہور"). For True/False write "True" or "False". For Fill in the blanks write the correct word(s). For Match the Column write the correct pairs. For Brief answers/subjective write a concise model answer.
6. **"blooms"**: Bloom's Taxonomy level. REQUIRED for EVERY question. Use one of: "Remember", "Understand", "Apply", "Analyze", "Evaluate", "Create".

BLOOM'S TAXONOMY ASSIGNMENT GUIDE

1. REMEMBER (Retrieval) — Retrieving a pre-stored fact exactly as learned.
2. UNDERSTAND (Translation/Interpretation) — Rephrasing or explaining a concept.
3. APPLY (Implementation) — Using a rule/formula on a new or specific case.
4. ANALYZE (Deconstruction) — Breaking a whole into parts to find patterns.
5. EVALUATE (Judgement/Critique) — Making a choice based on criteria.
6. CREATE (Synthesis) — Building something new by combining parts.

Pipeline Guardrails:
- Complexity ≠ Bloom Level. Do not confuse difficulty with cognitive level.
- The "Newness" Factor: scenario/case study not in training material → at least Apply.
- Verb Neutrality: Look at the object of the verb, not just the verb.
