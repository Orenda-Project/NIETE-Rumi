# Basics Build Spec — Number Fluency, Communicative Language, Phonics, Syllables, Concrete Maths

**Context.** The Teaching Calendar already schedules 405 "basics periods" — italic-coded slots (NF, CL, PH, AS/ارکان سازی, C) taught on whatever chapter the class is currently in, using that chapter's own words and numbers, so the class never leaves the book. Zero to partial lesson plans exist for these in the segment corpus today. This is the cheapest win available: the calendar slot exists, the pedagogy is anti-rote (exactly what leadership is asking for), and no new curriculum time has to be found — just content to fill time that's already reserved.

**Constraints baked into every template below:** Islamabad FDE government primary, 40+ children, one teacher, 40-minute periods, no materials budget beyond found objects (stones, bottle caps, chalk, fingers, scrap/torn paper) and the FDE-supplied textbook itself, many children below grade level, and teachers who are themselves often weak in English. One skill per lesson plan — no bundling.

---

## 1. Templates — one reusable 40-minute shape per basics type

Every template below runs **Open → Work → Share → Close**. What changes chapter to chapter is only the *content* (the chapter's own words/numbers); the *shape* stays fixed so a teacher who has run it once can run it cold the next week — critical at 40+ students where transition time is the enemy.

### A. Number Fluency (Maths NF)
- **Arc:** Chant warm-up (5 min) → guided fluency drill using this chapter's own numbers (20 min) → rapid individual/choral check (10 min) → chant close (5 min).
- **Talk split:** Teacher ~30% (models/calls), children ~70% (chorus, pair drill, individual recall). Fluency means child mouths moving, not teacher explaining.
- **Materials:** fingers, chalk on desk/board, stones/bottle caps as counters, the textbook's own numbers/page numbers as a free number source.
- **Anchor rule:** the number range or fact family drilled must come from numbers already appearing in this week's chapter — no new topic introduced, only faster recall of what the chapter already uses.

### B. Communicative Language (CL — English & Urdu)
- **Arc:** Teacher models a sentence frame/dialogue chorally (5 min) → structured pair talk at existing seats (20 min) → stand-at-desk share, 6-8 children (10 min) → chorus close (5 min).
- **Talk split:** Teacher ~20% (gives the frame, no lecturing), children ~80%. This is the anti-rote lever — the point is volume of child talk, not teacher content delivery.
- **Materials:** chalk to write the frame on the board; no props. The chapter's own key-word list supplies the vocabulary that fills the frame's blanks.
- **Anchor rule:** every sentence frame is built only from that week's chapter theme/vocabulary (a self-intro chapter yields "My name is ___, I feel ___ today"; a conversation chapter yields its own two-line exchange).

### C. Phonics (English PH)
- **Arc:** Sound/blend drill (5 min) → isolate-blend-read chapter key words on the board (20 min) → paired or row read-aloud check (10 min) → choral close (5 min).
- **Talk split:** Teacher ~40% (models the sound), children ~60% (chorus then individual read-aloud).
- **Materials:** chalk + blackboard, the chapter's own word list, finger air-writing, desk-tracing. Nothing printed beyond the textbook.
- **Anchor rule:** only words that actually appear in the current chapter's text are drilled — no outside phonics word lists, so a weak-in-English teacher only ever has to pronounce words already sitting in the book in front of them.

### D. ارکان سازی / Syllables (Urdu AS)
- **Arc:** Clap-count model on one chapter word (5 min) → build/segment more chapter words by clapping ارکان (20 min) → partner clap-check (10 min) → chant close (5 min).
- **Talk split:** same shape as Phonics — teacher models briefly, children do the repeated clapping/saying.
- **Materials:** claps, desk-taps, chalk to mark syllable breaks under words on the board, the chapter's own text as the only word source.
- **Anchor rule:** syllable-building only ever uses words drawn from that week's Urdu chapter (its title, its key vocabulary, or its riddle/rhyme lines).

### E. Concrete Maths (Maths C)
- **Arc:** Teacher models with real objects (5-8 min) → children manipulate found objects to solve the chapter's own numbers (20 min) → 2-3 children demonstrate their method (7-10 min) → close (5 min).
- **Talk split:** teacher demonstrates once, children's hands are on materials for 70%+ of the period.
- **Materials:** stones, bottle caps, twigs, torn scrap-paper strips (for fraction folding), body parts (hand-spans, footsteps) for measurement, chalk for board/floor diagrams.
- **Anchor rule:** the exact numbers/quantities in play are the chapter's own — concrete work represents the same numbers the textbook will ask for abstractly, immediately before or alongside the book page. The class never leaves the book; the objects are a bridge to it.

## 2. Worked examples — 3 per type, 15 total, each tied to a real chapter

### Number Fluency

**1. G1, Maths, Ch. 1 — "Number Buddies 0-9"** (target: number bonds to 10)
*Objective:* child gives the bond-partner for any number 0-9 (e.g. "6 needs 4") without recounting from 1, using only the chapter's own 0-9 range.
1. (0-5) Teacher calls a number 0-9; class chorally shouts its bond-partner to 10.
2. (5-10) Teacher models with stones: 6 stones in one box, asks how many more make 10, moves stones to show.
3. (10-20) Pairs each get 10 stones/bottle caps: partner A shows a pile, partner B shows the bond-partner pile; swap ~4 rounds.
4. (20-30) Teacher walks rows doing rapid individual oral bond-checks using the chapter's own page numbers as prompts.
5. (30-37) Whole-class "buddy chant," speeding up each round.
6. (37-40) Close: 3 children each state one bond pair as an exit check.
*Children do:* chant, move stones with a partner, answer individually.
*Success check:* 3 cold-called children each give the correct bond-partner within 3 seconds, no finger-recount from 1.

**2. G3, Maths, Ch. 3 — "Multiplication Mountain: Climbing the Numbers"** (target: 2/5/10-times fluency)
*Objective:* recall 2x/5x/10x facts (as introduced in this chapter) within 3 seconds, without skip-counting from 1.
1. (0-5) Chant the 5-times table forward and back, clapping each multiple.
2. (5-12) Teacher builds a stone array (e.g. 3 rows of 5) from a chapter word problem, asks for the multiplication fact.
3. (12-25) Pair drill: partner A taps out a row (e.g. 5 taps, 4 times), partner B calls the product; swap after 5 rounds.
4. (25-33) "Mountain climb": teacher calls a fact, a child who answers correctly gets a chalk step-mark on a board ladder.
5. (33-38) Rapid-fire by row on a clapped beat.
6. (38-40) Close: 2 facts as a thumbs-up/down exit check.
*Success check:* ≥80% of the class answers correctly and on-beat in the rapid-fire round.

**3. G5, Maths, Ch. 6 — "Decimal Diving: Exploring the Dot"** (target: mental estimation/rounding)
*Objective:* estimate a chapter decimal sum (e.g. 4.6 + 3.2) by rounding mentally before computing precisely.
1. (0-5) Teacher says a chapter decimal; class chorally rounds it to the nearest whole.
2. (5-15) Teacher writes two chapter decimals, models estimating the sum by rounding first, then contrasts with the book's exact method.
3. (15-27) Pairs work 4 chapter-sourced decimal problems: estimate aloud first, then write the exact answer in the book.
4. (27-33) Row cold-call: teacher reads a problem, row must shout the estimate within 5 seconds before computing.
5. (33-38) "Closest guess wins": 3 children estimate one problem, class checks which was closest once computed.
6. (38-40) Close: one new pair, one child estimates aloud.
*Success check:* on the closing question, most cold-called children estimate within 1 whole number of the true rounded value before computing exactly.

### Concrete Maths

**1. G1, Maths, Ch. 3 — "Sum and Difference Detectives"** (concrete addition/subtraction)
*Objective:* solve a chapter addition/subtraction fact with physical stones before writing the number sentence.
1. (0-5) Teacher models addition as a "detective" story with stone piles combined.
2. (5-10) Teacher models subtraction the same way, removing and counting remainder.
3. (10-25) Children (≈10 stones/caps each) solve 4 chapter word problems by building piles, then write the number sentence in the book/notebook.
4. (25-33) Partners swap and rebuild each other's piles to verify.
5. (33-38) Two "detective reports": children re-enact their solution at the front.
6. (38-40) Close: one more fact shown with stones, thumbs-up if the class agrees.
*Success check:* rebuilding a partner's problem gives the same answer as the textbook.

**2. G3, Maths, Ch. 6 — "Fraction Fiesta: A Party of Parts"** (concrete fraction folding)
*Objective:* physically fold a scrap-paper strip into the chapter's named fraction (e.g. 1/4) before marking the textbook diagram.
1. (0-5) Teacher folds a scrap strip into halves then quarters, naming each fold.
2. (5-12) Teacher matches the fold to the chapter's fraction diagram, shading the equivalent part.
3. (12-25) Children fold their own scrap strip (old exercise-book page) to a named fraction, shade the required part.
4. (25-33) Partners compare folds; teacher circulates.
5. (33-38) 3 children hold up a strip and name the fraction for the class to confirm.
6. (38-40) Close: strip tucked into the textbook page as a physical revision anchor.
*Success check:* on a random "show me 1/2" request, ≥80% of children produce the correct fold within 20 seconds.

**3. G4, Maths, Ch. 8 — "Measuring Masters: Length, Mass, and Capacity Quest"** (concrete measurement)
*Objective:* estimate and measure a chapter length/mass using hand-spans/footsteps/stone-balances, then compare to the book's standard-unit answer.
1. (0-5) Teacher models measuring the blackboard in hand-spans, counting aloud.
2. (5-12) Teacher links the hand-span count to the chapter's cm/m example.
3. (12-25) Pairs measure 3 objects (desk, notebook, doorframe) in body-units, and balance stones against a known object for mass; record in notebooks.
4. (25-33) Pairs compare counts with a neighbouring pair, discuss why they differ.
5. (33-38) Class estimates gathered on the board, then the chapter's standard-unit answer is revealed.
6. (38-40) Close: each child writes "my hand-span is about ___ cm."
*Success check:* each child records a measurement for ≥2 of 3 objects within a reasonable range of the class-agreed standard value.

### Phonics

**1. G1, English, Ch. 1 — "spiral the chapter's key words, self-intro, feelings, rhyme & days"** (initial-sound recognition)
*Objective:* say the initial sound of 5 chapter key words, then blend and read the whole word.
1. (0-5) Teacher writes 5 chapter key words, says each once, class repeats.
2. (5-12) Teacher isolates each initial sound, models blending sound-by-sound into the word.
3. (12-25) Individual children (by row) point to a board word matching a sound the teacher makes; class chorally sounds it out.
4. (25-33) Partners take turns pointing to one of the 5 words in their own textbook and sounding it out.
5. (33-38) Rapid "sound it, blend it" round, one child one word.
6. (38-40) Close: choral re-read of all 5, faster.
*Success check:* cold-called, ≥4 of 5 children independently blend and read one chapter key word.

**2. G2, English, Ch. 3 — "spiral all key skills of the chapter"** (consonant blends)
*Objective:* read chapter words containing a consonant blend (e.g. "st," "bl," "tr").
1. (0-5) Teacher writes 3-4 chapter blend words, underlines the blend, isolates then blends it.
2. (5-12) Teacher models finger-tapping each sound in the blend before blending into the word.
3. (12-25) Partners cover-and-reveal: one covers all but the blend, other reads it, then reads the full word; swap each word.
4. (25-33) Teacher points to a blend word in the textbook per row; individual child reads it.
5. (33-38) Row relay: fastest correct read of a random blend word.
6. (38-40) Close: choral re-read of today's blend words.
*Success check:* each child reads ≥1 blend word correctly in the individual check without the teacher sounding it first.

**3. G3, English, Ch. 4 — "spiral of blends, days/months, gender, verbs, MCQs & the travel diary"** (blend fluency in running text — this chapter names blends explicitly)
*Objective:* read a sentence from the chapter's travel-diary text fluently, pronouncing its blend words correctly.
1. (0-5) Teacher reads one diary sentence aloud, class echoes.
2. (5-12) Teacher isolates 2-3 blend words in it, drills isolate→blend→whole-word.
3. (12-25) Partners re-read the sentence, one listening for blend accuracy and giving thumbs-up/down.
4. (25-33) 6-8 individual children read the sentence aloud, one at a time.
5. (33-38) Whole class chants the day's trickiest blend word 5 times fast.
6. (38-40) Close: choral read of the full sentence at natural speed.
*Success check:* ≥6 of 8 individually-checked children read the sentence with correct blend pronunciation, uncorrected.

### ارکان سازی / Syllables (Urdu)

**1. G1, Urdu, Ch. 5 — "نھی چڑیا"** (clap-counting ارکان in chapter words)
*Objective:* clap the ارکان count of 5 chapter words and say each part-by-part.
1. (0-5) Teacher says چڑیا slowly with one clap per رکن; class copies.
2. (5-12) Teacher writes 4 more chapter words, models clap-count, chalk-dashes each رکن.
3. (12-25) Partners take turns: one says a chapter word slowly, other claps the count, then check the board together.
4. (25-33) Individual children clap-count one word each, spread across rows.
5. (33-38) Whole-class chant: teacher says a word, class claps then says it normally.
6. (38-40) Close: class claps the ارکان count of the chapter title itself.
*Success check:* given a new, un-drilled chapter word, ≥70% of called children clap the correct count.

**2. G1, Urdu, Ch. 8 — "چُنّوں مُنّوں"** (rhyme rhythm → syllable segmentation)
*Objective:* segment and clap the ارکان of 4-5 rhyming words matching the poem's own rhythm.
1. (0-5) Teacher recites 2 rhyme lines with a clear clap per رکن; class joins.
2. (5-12) Teacher isolates a rhyming word pair, claps each separately, compares counts.
3. (12-25) Partners recite a line together clapping, then try 2 more chapter words.
4. (25-33) Rows clap-recite one line each; teacher checks rhythm/accuracy.
5. (33-38) Whole class performs the full rhyme with claps.
6. (38-40) Close: 2 children clap just one word solo.
*Success check:* the two solo children clap the correct رکن count for their word with no lead-in.

**3. G2, Urdu, Ch. 7 — "پہیلی"** (segmenting/rebuilding riddle words)
*Objective:* break a 2-3 رکن chapter word into parts aloud and reassemble it.
1. (0-5) Teacher poses the chapter riddle; class listens for the answer word.
2. (5-12) Teacher writes the answer word plus 2-3 related words, segments each with chalk dashes, models part-by-part then whole.
3. (12-25) Partners take a word each: one segments aloud, other reassembles the whole word; swap.
4. (25-33) Individual check on 8-10 children, one word each.
5. (33-38) "Riddle relay": teacher gives a clue, first row to clap+say the answer's ارکان scores.
6. (38-40) Close: whole class says the day's key word slowly then fast.
*Success check:* each child in the individual check separates ≥1 chapter word into its correct parts.

### Communicative Language

**1. G1, English, Ch. 1 — self-intro sentence frame** ("My name is ___, I feel ___ today")
1. (0-5) Teacher models the frame on self; class chorally repeats with the teacher's name filled in.
2. (5-10) Teacher boards the frame with blanks, elicits 2-3 chapter feeling-words for the second blank.
3. (10-25) Shoulder-partner turn-talk: each child says the frame with their own name/feeling word; swap partners, repeat twice.
4. (25-33) "Stand and share": 6-8 named children stand at their own desk and say their sentence to the class.
5. (33-38) Chain drill: row by row, each child adds one new feeling-word to a class list on the board.
6. (38-40) Close: whole class choruses the frame once more with a made-up name.
*Success check:* each child who stands produces the full frame with their own name and a chapter feeling-word, unread.

**2. G2, Urdu, Ch. 5 — "گفتگو - اہم میٹنگ"** (turn-taking dialogue)
1. (0-5) Teacher reads the chapter's model conversation aloud, both parts, voice-changed.
2. (5-12) Teacher re-reads it; class choruses one character's line while teacher does the other, then swap.
3. (12-25) Shoulder partners run the two-line exchange 3 times, swapping roles each time.
4. (25-33) 3-4 pairs perform their exchange standing at their own desks for the row to hear.
5. (33-38) Teacher varies one word in the line; 2-3 pairs adapt their response live.
6. (38-40) Close: whole class choruses the full exchange as one voice.
*Success check:* ≥3 of the called pairs deliver the two-line exchange correctly and audibly, unprompted.

**3. G2, Urdu, Ch. 6 — "تقریر کا فن"** (structured mini-speech)
1. (0-5) Teacher delivers a 2-sentence model speech using the chapter's opening→point→closing structure.
2. (5-12) Teacher boards the 3-part frame with chapter vocabulary options for each blank.
3. (12-25) Children fill the frame with their own choice at their desks, then say it once to their shoulder partner.
4. (25-33) Row-based "mini stage": one child per row stands at their own desk and delivers to the row around them; rotate until 6-8 have spoken.
5. (33-38) Teacher repeats 2 strong examples as models for the whole class.
6. (38-40) Close: whole class claps the same rhythmic "thank you" closing line together.
*Success check:* ≥6 of 8 who stood produce all three parts, in order, using chapter vocabulary.

## 3. Number Fluency — the biggest single gap (101 scheduled, 0 built)

**Progression, G1 → G5:**

| Grade | Target skill |
|---|---|
| G1 | Counting, subitizing, number bonds to 10 (then 20) |
| G2 | Bonds to 20/100, doubling/halving, skip-counting by 2/5/10 |
| G3 | Times tables 2/5/10 (then 3/4) and related division facts |
| G4 | Full tables 2-12, factor/multiple recall, quick mental +/- with larger numbers |
| G5 | Estimation & rounding, mental strategies for decimals/percentages/fractions, quick equivalences (1/2 = 50% = 0.5) |

**Running the routine inside 40 minutes without displacing the chapter:** the NF period is *already* a dedicated calendar slot (not time carved out of a book lesson), so the design problem is how to structure that slot, not how to steal from another one. Keep a tight, **predictable 10-15 minute drill core** — chant, choral call-response, rapid-fire — bookending the period (first 5-10 min to warm up, last 5-10 min to close), with the middle 20-25 minutes as hands-on pair/individual application still using that week's chapter numbers (as in the worked examples above). The drill core's *shape* never changes week to week — only the numbers do — so a teacher runs it from memory by week 2, which is what makes it survivable at 40+ students with no aide.

## 4. Communicative Language — the engagement lever (95 periods, English + Urdu, 0 built)

Six routines that work in fixed rows with no floor space, each named for what it's for:

1. **Shoulder-Partner Turn-Talk** — turn to the child beside/behind, no movement. For daily sentence-frame drilling (2-line exchange, fill-in-the-blank frame).
2. **Row Relay / Chain Drill** — going down a row, each child adds one word/phrase to a growing chain. For building a class word-bank aloud with only one voice active at a time — keeps 40+ children orderly.
3. **Stand-at-Desk Mini-Stage** — a child stands *at their own desk* to speak to the row/neighbours, instead of walking to the front (which eats transition time at scale). Teacher rotates which rows present each day so everyone gets a turn across the week.
4. **Echo & Choral Response** — teacher models, whole class choruses back. Front-loads vocabulary/structure before pair work; gives 40+ voices a way to be "on" without individual calling.
5. **Same-Bench Buzz (think-pair-share, no movement)** — desk partners on a shared bench (standard FDE furniture) buzz for 60-90 seconds on a prompt, then 3-4 pairs report out. Uses existing seating, zero reorganizing.
6. **Call-and-Respond Chant** — a rhythmic group chant of the chapter's key phrase. Energizer, and gives shy/weak children cover to practice pronunciation inside a group voice before being asked to speak solo.

## 5. Build order and effort (405 periods)

Ranked by impact (size of gap × how directly it serves the anti-rote/engagement ask) crossed with cheapness (how templatable/reusable the content generation is):

| Tier | Type | Missing | Why this rank | Rough sizing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Number Fluency (Maths NF) | 101 | Biggest single gap, 0 built, simplest template (chant/drill), directly the leadership ask | 3-day pilot (G1+G3, ~20 LPs) to validate template, then ~2 weeks to scale to all 101 |
| **2** | Communicative Language, Eng + Urdu (CL) | 55 + 40 = 95 | 0 built, #2 engagement priority; needs chapter-vocabulary curation per frame but the routine shape reuses | ~2-3 weeks, can run in parallel with Tier 1 once template is locked |

| **4** | Concrete Maths (C) | 65 | Needs a manipulatives-mapping pass per chapter (stones/folding/measuring workaround varies by topic) — same Maths team as Tier 1, sequenced right after for continuity | ~1.5-2 weeks |

**Total: 405.** With a small team (2-3 content writers + 1 reviewer) running Maths (NF → C → WP) and Language (CL → PH/AS) tracks with partial overlap, the two highest-priority, zero-built types (NF + CL = 196 periods) clear in roughly 3-4 weeks, and the full 405 in about 8-9 weeks.

**Monday start:** kick off the Tier 1 pilot (20 NF lesson plans, G1 + G3) against the templates in §1 and the worked examples in §2 — small enough to review fast, big enough to prove the template survives a real classroom before committing to all 101.

## 6. Quality bar — checklist before a generated basics lesson ships

1. **Chapter-anchored** — every word/number/example is traceable to that week's chapter's own text or numbers; zero outside vocabulary introduced.
2. **One skill only** — the LP addresses exactly one basics type (NF/CL/PH/AS/C); no bundling two skills into one period.
3. **Materials check** — every material named is on the free/found list (stones, bottle caps, chalk, fingers, torn scrap paper, the textbook); no purchase, no printing beyond the FDE-supplied book.
4. **Talk-ratio check** — children's active talk/response time is the majority of the period, per the template's split; not teacher-lecture dominant.
5. **Works at 40+ desks in fixed rows** — no activity requires floor space, a circle, or moved furniture; all pair/group work uses existing shoulder/bench partners.
6. **Below-grade-level accessible** — at least one move lets a child below the expected level participate without having already mastered the target skill (choral response or partner modeling comes before any solo ask).
7. **Weak-English-teacher safe** (English CL/PH especially) — the teacher-facing script gives the exact model sentence/sound to say aloud, not an instruction to "explain"; scripted so a teacher can read it cold.
8. **Observable success check** — the closing check is verifiable by the teacher in the room in under 2 minutes (cold-call count, thumbs-up/down, clap-count accuracy), never a written test requiring take-home grading.
